The problem

You spend most of your day inside.

Habit trackers remind you to go outside. None of them tell you whether right now is actually a good time — or whether 4pm, when the UV drops and the air clears up, would serve you a lot better.

Fresh Air doesn't ask for willpower.
It just tells you when the sky is worth stepping into.

How it works

Four small things, every day.

No workouts, no goals, no step counts — just a genuine daily habit of being outside, reflected honestly back at you.

  1. Find the window

    Temperature, UV, and air quality across the day, scored for actual comfort — not just whichever hour is warmest.

  2. Step outside

    A nudge lands right as your window opens. Two minutes or twenty — Fresh Air isn't keeping score of duration.

  3. One photo

    Sky, clouds, rain — whatever's actually out there. One tap on how you feel, and you're done.

  4. Watch it add up

    A streak that reflects a real habit — with a freeze you earn, not a guilt trip, on the days that don't go to plan.

Why it's different

Built around real conditions, not reminders.

Weather-first, not reminder-first

Every recommendation is scored against live temperature, UV index, and air quality — the same signals a person would actually check before heading out.

One photo, no performance

No filters, no feed, no likes. A single daily photo of the sky is proof enough — for you, not an audience.

A streak that forgives

Every 7-day streak banks a freeze — a real day off, not a broken chain. The habit survives being human.
